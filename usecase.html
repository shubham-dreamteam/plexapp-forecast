How a Plex ad deal actually flows.

Four parties, two date dimensions, one pilot. The play between Advertiser, Brand, Agency and Plex — and what the CRM has to model to make it work.

01 · ENTITY MODELThe four parties

Plex sells the inventory. The Agency holds the budget relationship and signs the IO. The Brand is what gets advertised. The Advertiser is the legal entity that owns the brand (often a holdco that owns several).

02 · THE WIRINGIt's many-to-many on both sides

A brand can be repped by multiple agencies simultaneously — different media disciplines or different geographies. An agency reps a portfolio of brands. The CRM has to label every one of these edges with a role.

One brand → many agencies

One agency → many brands

03 · THE TWO-DATE PROBLEMClose date ≠ revenue date

An IO can close in May but the ads might not run until Q4. Forecasting on close date will lie about both quarters. The CRM has to carry deal-close + flight-start + flight-end and split revenue across the flight window.

WHAT THE CRM HAS TO MODEL

The pilot data model in one screen

  • Brand and Agency as distinct first-class company records.
  • Brand ↔ Agency edge with a role label (media / creative / geo).
  • Deal ↔ one Agency (paper-holder) and ↔ one+ Brands (advertised).
  • Flight as a child of Deal: start, end, line items, per-quarter revenue split.
  • Contacts threaded on both sides — agency planner + brand marketing owner.
  • SKU tagging on line items so revenue rolls up to the advertiser holdco.
